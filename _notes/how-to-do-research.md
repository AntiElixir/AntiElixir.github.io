Reflections on doing research as an undergraduate, based on my experience with ocean science and AI projects.

## Getting Started

### Finding a Topic

1. **Talk to professors.** They know the open problems and can guide you.
2. **Read recent papers.** Look for "future work" sections — these are often accessible entry points.
3. **Start small.** Don't try to solve everything. Pick one specific question.

### Building Background

1. **Read systematically.** Start with review papers, then key references, then recent work.
2. **Take notes.** Use a system (Obsidian, Notion, etc.) to organize what you learn.
3. **Reproduce results.** Try to replicate simple experiments from papers. This builds intuition.

## The Research Process

### 1. Define the Question

Be specific. "Understanding ocean eddies" is too broad. "How do cyclonic eddies affect chlorophyll concentration in the North Indian Ocean during summer monsoon?" is better.

### 2. Gather Data

- Identify data sources early
- Check data availability and quality
- Plan for data cleaning and preprocessing

### 3. Explore and Analyze

- Start with simple visualizations
- Look for patterns before building complex models
- Document everything — you will forget what you did

### 4. Iterate

Research is not linear. You will go back and forth between:
- Reading more papers
- Trying new approaches
- Refining your question

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Perfectionism.** Don't wait until everything is perfect to share your work.
2. **Isolation.** Talk to others. Explain your work. Get feedback.
3. **Scope creep.** Stay focused on your main question.
4. **Imposter syndrome.** Everyone feels this. Keep going.

## Tools I Use

- **Python:** Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, Xarray
- **Obsidian:** For note-taking and knowledge management
- **Git:** For version control
- **LaTeX:** For writing papers

## Advice for Beginners

1. **It's okay to not know.** Research is about learning.
2. **Ask questions.** Professors and senior students expect this.
3. **Celebrate small wins.** Finishing a data cleaning pipeline is progress.
4. **Take breaks.** Burnout is real. Rest is productive.
