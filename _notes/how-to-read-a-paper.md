Reading papers efficiently is a crucial skill for any researcher. Here's my approach, inspired by S. Keshav's classic guide.

## The Three-Pass Approach

### First Pass: The Bird's-Eye View (5-10 minutes)

Read the title, abstract, and introduction. Look at the section headings. Read the conclusions. Glance at the references to see what you already know.

**Goal:** Decide if this paper is worth reading further. Ask yourself: What is this paper about? Is it relevant to my work?

### Second Pass: The Content (1 hour)

Read the paper more carefully, but skip the detailed proofs or derivations. Look at the figures and diagrams carefully. Mark relevant references for later reading.

**Goal:** Understand the main contributions and methodology. Be able to summarize the paper to someone else.

### Third Pass: The Deep Dive (several hours)

Read the paper thoroughly, including all proofs and derivations. Try to reproduce the results mentally. Think about what you would do differently.

**Goal:** Fully understand the paper. Identify strengths, weaknesses, and potential extensions.

## My Personal Tips

1. **Keep a reading log.** Write down the key idea, strengths, weaknesses, and questions for each paper.
2. **Connect to your own work.** How does this paper relate to what you're doing? What can you borrow?
3. **Don't read linearly.** Jump between sections as needed. The introduction and conclusion often tell you most of what you need to know.
4. **Read the references.** If a paper cites something you don't know, go read that too. Build your knowledge tree.

## Questions to Ask

- What problem does this paper solve?
- Why is this problem important?
- What is the key idea or technique?
- What are the assumptions and limitations?
- What would I do next?
