Notes from reading 《人工智能极简史》. A concise overview of AI development from Turing to deep learning.

## Key Insights

### The Fast vs. Slow Thinking Paradox

The brainstem, responsible for fast, intuitive thinking, has a long evolutionary history. The cerebral cortex, responsible for slow, logical thinking, evolved much more recently. AI excels at the latter but struggles with the former. Interestingly, AI researchers focus little on the fast thinking that comes so naturally to humans.

### "Building Airplanes vs. Designing Birds"

To create truly intelligent agents with human-like consciousness, we may need a completely different technical route—one that doesn't rely solely on compute, big data, and large models.

## Timeline Highlights

### 1936-1955: The萌芽期 (Germination)

- **1936:** Turing machine — abstract computation model
- **1950:** Turing Test — behavioral standard for intelligence
- **1943:** McCulloch-Pitts neuron model — starting point of neural networks
- **1946:** ENIAC — first general-purpose electronic computer
- **1948:** Cybernetics (Wiener) and Information Theory (Shannon)

### 1956-1980: The初创期 (Founding)

- **1956:** Dartmouth Conference — birth of AI as a field
- **1959:** Samuel coins "Machine Learning"
- **1966:** ALPAC report criticizes machine translation, funding cuts
- **1976:** Physical Symbol System Hypothesis (Newell & Simon)

### Key Debates

- **Chinese Room (Searle, 1980):** Syntax manipulation ≠ semantic understanding
- **Symbolism vs. Connectionism:** The ongoing tension between rule-based and neural approaches

## Reflection

The history of AI is full of cycles of hype and disappointment. Each "AI winter" was followed by a new spring, often driven by unexpected breakthroughs. The current deep learning era may face similar challenges — what comes next?
