Mesoscale eddies are rotating bodies of water with diameters of 50-300 km and lifetimes of weeks to months. They play a crucial role in ocean dynamics and climate.

## Basic Properties

### Size and Distribution

- **Diameter:** Typically 50-300 km, varying with latitude
- **Latitude dependence:** Eddy diameter decreases with increasing latitude, likely due to dissipation effects
- **Hotspots:** Western boundary currents (Gulf Stream, Kuroshio), Antarctic Circumpolar Current

### Amplitude and SSH

- Sea surface height (SSH) variation correlates with eddy activity
- Higher SSH variance → more tracked eddies, higher eddy density and amplitude
- **Exception:** Eastern subtropical regions (South Pacific, North Atlantic) have abundant eddies but small SSH standard deviation

### Polarity

- **Cyclonic eddies:** Counterclockwise rotation (Northern Hemisphere), upwelling, cold core
- **Anticyclonic eddies:** Clockwise rotation (Northern Hemisphere), downwelling, warm core
- No strong preference for either polarity in most regions

- Chelton et al. (2011): Global observations of nonlinear mesoscale eddies
